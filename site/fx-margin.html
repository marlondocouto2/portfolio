Project 3 · Risk

Currency profit and risk

The problem: a money-transfer company quotes customers an exchange rate with a small fee built in (the markup), then buys the currency a day or two later. If the currency moves in between, the profit on the transfer shrinks, sometimes below zero.

What I built: a forecast of each day's profit margin, in two steps: a trend, then machine learning to correct what the trend misses. Plus a "bad day" estimate: how low the margin could go on 1 day in 20.

The result: the machine-learning step removes 83% of the error that can be predicted at all, and the bad-day estimate holds on 94% of days, as designed. It uses real exchange rates for Mexico, India and Brazil.

What I learned

  • Most of the miss can't be predicted by anyone. Exchange rates move almost randomly over a day or two. That's the dashed "best possible" line: no model can get below it.
  • Machine learning closes most of the gap that's left. The trend only sees margins a few days old, so it lags. The company already knows today's markup, and using it brings the forecast up to date.
  • Knowing the bad day matters as much as the average. The bad-day estimate tells the company how much it could lose on a rough day, per route. Brazil, the most volatile currency, gets the widest range.

For the technical reader

  1. Data: real FX rates from FRED (peso, rupee, real and the dollar index, 2016–2026), with a simulated business on top: a markup that widens in volatile markets, and settlement one or two business days later.
  2. Stage 1: an EWMA trend of margins that have certainly settled, with a 28-day moving average for comparison.
  3. Stage 2: Ridge, XGBoost and LightGBM learn stage 1's error from FX moves, volatility, the dollar index, today's markup and the calendar. Tuned with Optuna on time-ordered cross-validation.
  4. Margin at risk: LightGBM quantile regression for the 5th percentile, with a conformal correction checked on the last 180 days of training data.
  5. Evaluation: a walk-forward test over two years, refitting every 30 days on settled data only, with SHAP to explain the corrections. The "best possible" line is an oracle that knows the markup but not the FX move.

The full method with formulas is in the methodology. Volumes come from the Project 2 generator.
