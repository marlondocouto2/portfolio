Project 3 · Margin at risk

Two-stage FX margin forecaster

A money-transfer business quotes each customer yesterday's exchange rate minus a markup, then buys the currency a day or two later at whatever the rate is by then. If the currency moves against it in between, the margin shrinks or turns negative. This project forecasts each corridor's daily margin and its margin at risk: a trend first, then machine learning on what the trend misses.

What the results show

  • Most of the error can't be forecast. About 53 of 57 basis points is the currency's move during settlement. FX rates behave close to a random walk, so the "oracle" line, which knows the markup but not the FX move, is the floor no model can beat.
  • Stage 2 captures most of what can be. The trend only sees margins at least 4 days old, so it lags; the business already knows today's markup. Bringing the trend up to date with that is most of what stage 2 does.
  • The value is in risk as much as accuracy. A calibrated 5% band tells treasury how much margin could be lost on a bad day, per corridor. Brazil, the most volatile currency, gets the widest band.

How it works

  1. Real FX rates from FRED (peso, rupee, real and the dollar index, 2016–2026), with a simulated business on top: a markup that widens in volatile markets, and settlement one or two business days later.
  2. Stage 1: an EWMA trend of margins that have certainly settled, with a 28-day moving average for comparison.
  3. Stage 2: Ridge, XGBoost and LightGBM learn stage 1's error from FX moves, volatility, the dollar index, today's markup and the calendar. Tuned with Optuna on time-ordered cross-validation.
  4. Margin at risk: LightGBM quantile regression for the 5th percentile, with a conformal correction checked on the last 180 days of training data.
  5. Walk-forward test over two years, refitting every 30 days on settled data only, with SHAP to explain the corrections.

The full method with formulas is in the methodology. Volumes come from the Project 2 generator.
