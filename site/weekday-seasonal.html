Weekday-seasonal forecaster

A treasury team funds each day's payments before the day starts. Too low and they're short; too high and cash sits idle. This model forecasts daily volume 1–30 days ahead with one sentence: recent level × day-of-week effect × payday effect.

Why the simple model wins

  • The moving average ignores the weekly pattern. It predicts nearly the same number every day, so it's always low on Fridays and paydays and high on weekends.
  • Repeating last week copies last week's noise and puts paydays on the wrong dates: last Tuesday may have been the 1st, this Tuesday is the 8th.
  • The seasonal model estimates each effect from many days, so only today's noise remains. Its error is close to the 6% noise built into the data, and it holds up to 30 days ahead.
  • What it misses: the December peak, because it has no yearly component. That, plus spikes and holidays, is what Project 2 fixes.

How it works

  1. Weekday factors, last 12 weeks: each day divided by the average of the week around it, so growth and slow seasonal bumps cancel out.
  2. Payday factors, last year: the same idea for five day-of-month buckets (1–3, 4–13, 14–16, 17 to month end, last 3 days).
  3. The two are estimated in turn for three passes, because a payday on a Friday would otherwise inflate Friday.
  4. Level: the average of the last 28 days after removing both effects. Forecast = level × weekday factor × payday factor.

Data: one simulated series, $50M a day growing 15% a year, with known weekday, payday and December effects and 6% noise. The full method with formulas is in the methodology.
