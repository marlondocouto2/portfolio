Project 2 · Forecasting

Handling unusual days

The problem: the Project 1 forecast works well on ordinary days, but three kinds of day break it: random spikes (a one-off large payment), payroll days (every other Friday) and holidays (a rush before, a drop on the day).

What I built: five versions, each fixing one kind of day, all tested the same way, on three money-transfer routes from the U.S.: to Mexico, the Philippines and India.

The result: 38% more accurate than Project 1, so the company can keep $10.5M less cash on hand a day.

Bonus: does a ready-made AI model do better?

Added later, after reading recent research.

I also tried Chronos-2, a free AI forecasting model from Amazon that was trained on many other datasets. I used it as-is: it never learned from this data, it just reads each day's history and forecasts. It got the same calendar information as my versions (weekday, paydays and holidays).

  • On these routes, my final version wins. Chronos-2 is good on ordinary days but poor on holidays: five years of data hold too few of each holiday for it to learn from.
  • On real U.S. Treasury data, Chronos-2 wins for next-day forecasts. Real data is messier than my simulated routes, and that's where a model trained on many datasets helps.

All the numbers

What I learned

  • Fix one kind of day at a time. Each fix helps exactly the days it targets, so it's clear what every step is worth.
  • Random spikes do damage even on normal days. They inflate the average the forecast is built on. Setting them aside makes ordinary days more accurate.
  • Repeating events should be predicted, not ignored. The payroll run every other Friday is big but regular. A model that learns repeating cycles forecasts it; the earlier versions couldn't.

For the technical reader

  1. Baseline: the Project 1 model, recent level × weekday factor × payday factor.
  2. + Spike GMM: a Gaussian mixture flags random spikes in the residuals; the baseline is refitted without them.
  3. + Holidays: holidays are cleaned out too, then each holiday's effect (surge, drop, catch-up) is learned from past years and multiplied back in.
  4. Prophet + Fourier: Fourier seasonalities (weekly, every 14 days, monthly, yearly) treat the payroll run as seasonality, so it gets forecast.
  5. Prophet + Fourier + GMM: the mixture runs on Prophet's residuals, so it removes only the spikes the seasonality can't explain.
  6. Chronos-2: a 120M-parameter pretrained model used zero-shot, with weekday, payday and holiday-window covariates.

Evaluation: a walk-forward backtest over 2025, 1, 7 and 30 days ahead. Data: three simulated routes with known weekday, payday, holiday and spike effects, plus public U.S. Treasury data. The full method with formulas is in the methodology.
