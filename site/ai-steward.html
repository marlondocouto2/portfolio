AI model reviewer

The problem: a forecasting model can go stale as markets change. Someone has to check it regularly and decide whether to keep it or replace it, and that takes a data scientist's time every week.

What I built: an AI (Claude) that does the weekly review of the Project 3 model. It looks at what changed, tests a few alternatives and recommends keep or replace. A fixed safety rule then decides whether a replacement is allowed (the AI can't change that rule), and a person gives the final approval.

The result: starting from a weak model, the AI improved it the most of four methods, with half the work. Starting from a strong model, it rightly left it alone. It's an early result: 16 weeks tested.

The safety rule

The AI can recommend a replacement, but only this rule can approve it.

  • The last 8 weeks are kept hidden from the reviewer while it experiments, so it can't simply pick whatever fits recent data best.
  • Once a week, its best candidate is tested on those hidden weeks. It must be at least 1% better, very unlikely to be better by luck, and give a believable bad-day range.
  • If any check fails, the current model stays. A person still approves every replacement.
Starting modelWhat the rule did
StrongProtected it. Most proposed replacements were luck, and would have made forecasts up to 1.07% worse. The rule blocked them.
WeakToo cautious. The replacements were real improvements (6–7% better), but 8 weeks is rarely enough evidence, so the rule let only a quarter to a third of that gain through.

What I learned

  • A good model is hard to beat, and blind automation makes it worse. Picking the best of a dozen options on past data mostly picks the luckiest one.
  • The AI's edge is judgement: knowing when to leave a model alone, and what to try when it's weak. It used about half the tests of the other methods.
  • The safety rule needs to adapt. It protected a good model but held back real gains for a weak one. A rule that waits for more evidence would fix that.
  • It cost nothing to run. The AI runs on an existing Claude plan, with no extra bill.

For the technical reader

  1. Tools: a command-line interface (python -m steward ...) for status, data summary, champion report, diagnose, backtest, tune, promote and submit. Every result goes into a ledger with an id.
  2. Guardrails: a fixed config space (4 model families, 6 feature groups, training windows, refit frequency, EWMA half-life), a weekly budget of 15 backtests, 2 tunes and 1 promotion test, and memo checks (numbers must match the ledger).
  3. Promotion rule: on the hidden 8 weeks, at least 1% lower MAE, a one-sided Diebold–Mariano p-value below 0.10, and a 5% margin-at-risk band breached on 2–9% of days.
  4. Reviewers: never retrain, a scripted playbook, Optuna over the whole config space, and Claude Code running headless with written instructions, in a sandbox whose data stops at the cutoff.
  5. Evaluation: each Friday from January 2024 to August 2026 replayed from the same starting champion; the chosen model is scored on the following 12 weeks. The AI ran on 16 weeks sampled from calm, normal and volatile markets.

The model and data come from Project 3, installed as a package. Full details are in the README.
